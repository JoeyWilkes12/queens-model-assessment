## Question and design

This bounded follow-up revisits six model/board pairs selected because the preceding study observed mathematical failures: GPT-4.1 on all three boards, Claude Sonnet 4 on 9×9, and Gemini 2.5 Flash on 5×5 and 7×7. Each pair is tested with the original image, a complete region matrix, and JSON region-cell lists. Each pair-condition receives two independent fresh one-shot requests. These are two separate samples, not a two-attempt solving session; there is no best-of-two score.

Strict success requires the exact JSON-only schema and every Queens rule. Written success deterministically extracts exactly one schema-shaped JSON object from visible final text and applies the same rule checker. It does not repair coordinates, choose among alternatives, or override strict success. Both measures are reported separately.

The previous study’s two text probes remain historical exploratory observations. They are excluded from this matched 36-request denominator and are not pooled with these results. The previous report’s Claude 9×9 matrix omission described the budget situation when that earlier $2 phase was frozen; the new separately authorized $5 stage now includes Claude Sonnet 4 on 9×9 twice in each representation. The earlier study’s content, outcomes, costs, and exploratory calls are unchanged.

## Interpretation limits

- The cohort is failure-selected: it characterizes only these six previously failed model/board pairs, not the broader model or board population.
- Two observations per condition support only a limited repeatability check. They do not establish stable accuracy, statistical significance, generation thresholds, or a best-of-two solving rate. Keep both replicates visible.
- Text inputs provide a complete region partition, while images require the model to perceive it. Modality, wording, serialization, and token count change together; a text advantage cannot isolate visual perception as the cause.
- Counterbalanced order and fresh image controls reduce some temporal confounding, but these remain service-level observations. Routes, returned versions, queueing, and sampling variability may change.
- The largest recorded between-request pause was 146.89 minutes, between saved trial timestamps. It is excluded from request latency, not attributed to a model, and increases the possibility of service/time drift despite unchanged frozen inputs. This was not an uninterrupted benchmark session.
- No candidate answers, solution hints, correctness feedback, prior responses, tools, retries, or effort sweep were used.
